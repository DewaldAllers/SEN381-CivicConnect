# Forward Engineering Considerations

Version 0.1 working draft | 9 September 2026 | Owner: Tristan Els | Status: **draft for team review, not an approved baseline**

SRC-M1 section 4 (p. 4) asks for five to seven later lifecycle concerns that already affect the current requirements, constraints, risks or assumptions. Seven are recorded here. Each states why it matters now, which later activity it affects, what information is still missing, and what happens if it is ignored.

These are concerns, not decisions. SRC-M1 section 5 (pp. 4-5) keeps architecture, stack, schema, UI, API, CI and production deployment out of M1, and section 4 (p. 4) says deferring a decision is acceptable when the team can state what evidence is still needed. Nothing here selects a technology.

The concerns were chosen because each one already changes something the team writes down in M1. A concern that only becomes real in M3, and changes nothing now, was left out. The last section lists what was considered and not selected.

## FEC-004 resolved in draft by proposal P-02

When this register was written on 9 September, FR-009 and FR-010 contradicted each other and no rule covered a request with no due date. Part 2 closed the gap the same day. Proposal P-02 now states that a request is overdue when its due date has passed and its status is Accepted or In Progress, and that requests without a due date are shown separately as "due date not set". FR-010 cites P-02 as the source of the overdue meaning, so a reader can follow the rule from the requirement.

This is a draft resolution, not a closed concern. P-02 is one of the five proposals still awaiting team confirmation, which is [RSK-005](../risks/README.md). Working hours against calendar time and the time zone of the record are still unstated, and both change what the report computes. The concern stays open on that basis.

Recording it here shows the sequence: Part 3 found a defect in a Part 2 artefact through cross-review, Part 2 fixed it in the same milestone, and the register was updated to say so.

## The two that most constrain Milestone 2

FEC-001 and FEC-004 are the two that change M2 work directly, not later.

FEC-001 matters because the word "authorised" already appears across five requirements and currently means nothing specific. Until P-03 defines the roles and their visibility, the team cannot design a data boundary, and NFR-001 promises a permission test matrix whose rows are not yet known. The order matters: roles first, then the data model, then enforcement. Reversing that order is what produces authorisation checks in the user interface, which is the failure the entry describes.

FEC-004 mattered because it was a contradiction inside the current requirements, not a missing future decision. FR-010 required overdue reporting while FR-009 made the due date optional, so one of them had to give. It was caught while both were still drafts, and Part 2 closed it the same day through P-02. Had it survived to M3 it would have cost a change request against a baselined requirement, with the impact analysis SRC-MASTER section 14 (p. 13) requires. What remains open is narrower: whether overdue counts working hours or calendar time, and which time zone the record uses.

## Considered and not selected

SRC-M1 section 4 (p. 4) gives scalability, maintainability and operational cost as example categories. They were considered and left out of the register, for reasons the team should challenge if it disagrees.

Scalability was not selected because the brief describes one community organisation and gives no volume figures. The ten concurrent users in NFR-005 are a test assumption, not a demand forecast, and Part 2 says so. Treating scale as a concern now would mean designing against a number nobody has established. It should be revisited when Part 1 produces stakeholder evidence about the size of the organisation.

Maintainability was not selected as a separate entry because at this stage it has no specific content beyond the general aim of writing clear code. It becomes concrete in M2 once there is an architecture to evaluate, and M3 carries a technical debt register.

Operational cost was not selected separately because it is already inside FEC-006. Splitting it would produce two entries pointing at the same missing information, which is the free-tier research nobody has done.
